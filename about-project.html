Cholera & Civic Belfast is a public history project exploring the impact of cholera epidemics on nineteenth-century Belfast and the development of public health responses within the city.

The project is based on doctoral research into cholera in Belfast between 1832 and 1878, and draws on a wide range of historical sources including official reports, newspapers, medical accounts, and civic records. It examines how repeated epidemic outbreaks shaped urban infrastructure, medical practice, and civic reform.

The site is organised around cholera epidemics, public health developments, biographical studies, and narrative research essays. Together, these sections provide a structured account of how disease and reform shaped nineteenth-century Belfast.

The aim is to present research in an accessible format that bridges academic scholarship and public history, allowing wider audiences to engage with the social and medical history of nineteenth-century Belfast.

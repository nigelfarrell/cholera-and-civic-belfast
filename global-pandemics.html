First Pandemic (1817–1824)

Origin: Bengal (Ganges Delta)
Reach: Asia, East Africa

Transmission routes: Trade routes across India, Southeast Asia, Middle East.

Key developments: First documented global expansion of cholera beyond South Asia.

No direct Belfast outbreak recorded in this phase.

Second Pandemic (1829–1837)

Origin: India → Russia → Europe
Reach: Europe, UK, Ireland, Americas

Transmission routes: Overland caravan routes and maritime trade networks.

Key developments: Arrival in Europe (1831–32); establishment of cholera as a British public health crisis.

Includes: 1832 Belfast cholera epidemic

Third Pandemic (1846–1860)

Origin: Bengal → global maritime spread
Reach: Worldwide

Transmission routes: Shipping routes, military movement, and rapidly expanding port cities.

Key developments: Peak global mortality; John Snow’s 1854 epidemiological investigations.

Includes: 1848–49 and 1854 Belfast epidemics

Fourth Pandemic (1863–1875)

Origin: Bengal
Reach: Global (uneven impact)

Transmission routes: Steamship expansion enabling faster global diffusion.

Key developments: Improved European sanitation reduces mortality impact, but outbreaks persist.

Includes: 1866 Belfast cholera outbreak
